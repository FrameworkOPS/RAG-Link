Biggest Utilization Gap: JobNimbus
JobNimbus has scheduling, job management, workflow automation, and task management built in — all features that Summit Roofing is currently solving with Google Sheets, Monday.com, and manual processes.
Activating these features eliminates 2 redundant tools and the scheduling spreadsheet. Estimated time to fully configure: 8–12 hrs with a structured implementation plan.
$624/yr eliminated (Monday) ~4 hrs/wk Sheets work eliminated
Highest-Impact Unused Feature: QBO Job Costing
QBO's class tracking is set up but not enforced. Job costing by class requires every expense and invoice to be tagged — currently only ~30% are. Without clean job cost data, you can't tell which job types are actually profitable.
At $4.2M revenue with typical roofing margins, a 2–3% GM improvement from better job costing visibility is $84K–$126K annually. This requires process discipline, not more software.
Redundancy Findings
3 redundant tool pairs identified. $4,200/yr in immediate savings available with no functionality lost.
🔴 Redundancy #1: EagleView + Hover (aerial measurement)
Both tools do aerial measurement and material quantity takeoffs. Team defaults to EagleView — Hover was trialed during Q3 last year and never cancelled. Usage logs show Hover used 8 times in the last 6 months, all for simple measurements that EagleView handles. No unique Hover capability is being used.
Annual waste: $1,788/yr → Cancel Hover immediately
🟡 Redundancy #2: Monday.com + JobNimbus Tasks (task/project tracking)
Monday.com is used by the office manager for internal task tracking. JobNimbus has a full task and project management feature set that is currently unused. Moving internal tasks to JobNimbus consolidates the stack, keeps everything in the same system the team is already in, and eliminates a $48/month tool. Migration effort: approximately 2 hours.
Annual waste: $576/yr → Cancel Monday.com, migrate to JobNimbus tasks
🟡 Redundancy #3: Google Forms + JobNimbus Lead Intake (lead capture)
The website uses a Google Form for lead capture. The lead data is then manually re-entered into JobNimbus as a new contact. JobNimbus has a native web form / lead capture feature that feeds directly into the CRM with no manual re-entry. This double-entry takes office staff approximately 20 minutes per lead — at 15 leads/week, that's 5 hours/week of avoidable data entry.
Annual waste: ~$19,500 (staff time, not software cost) → Replace Google Form with JobNimbus web form
Communication Channel Overlap (Partial Redundancy)
Field comms happen across 3 channels simultaneously: Slack (office staff prefer), group text threads (field crews prefer), and phone calls (owner defaults). No single source of record for job-related field communication. This isn't a software cost problem — it's a process problem that software can't fix until you standardize the communication protocol first.
Recommendation: Standardize field comms on a single channel before investing in FSM. The channel doesn't matter as much as consistency. Once an FSM is in place, the field messaging typically consolidates naturally.

FSM Decision Framework (6–12 Month Horizon)
At $4.2M revenue with 22 employees, Summit Roofing is at the inflection point where a purpose-built FSM platform starts delivering ROI over a patched stack. The decision isn't urgent — Q3–Q4 is the right window, after Phase 1 consolidation is stable.

Jobber ($200–$350/mo): Right fit for $2M–$8M roofing. Handles scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, client management, and field app in one platform. Lower implementation overhead than ServiceTitan. Would replace JobNimbus, Monday.com, and the scheduling spreadsheet entirely.

ServiceTitan ($400–$800/mo): More powerful but significantly higher implementation cost and onboarding time (3–6 months). Better fit at $8M+ or when scaling past 40 field employees. Not the right next step at this revenue level.

Don't make this decision until: JobNimbus is fully activated and job data is clean in QBO for 90+ days. You need clean data to migrate to an FSM — migrating chaos just moves the problem.
