# SOP Framework for Home Service Companies

## Why SOPs Fail (and How to Avoid It)

Most home service companies that have tried to build SOPs end up with a Google Drive folder full of documents no one reads. The failure mode is almost always the same: too long, too complex, not trained to anyone, and not tied to accountability.

An SOP is only valuable if three conditions are met:
1. The person doing the task knows it exists
2. They can find it in under 60 seconds
3. They are held accountable to following it

This framework addresses all three.

---

## What to Document First

Do not try to document everything at once. Start with the highest-frequency, highest-stakes processes — the ones that, if done inconsistently, cause the most customer complaints, revenue loss, or employee frustration.

**Priority order for most home service companies:**

1. **Dispatch / Job Scheduling** — How a job gets from booked to on the board correctly
2. **Estimate Process** — How an estimate is run, priced, and delivered
3. **Job Closeout** — What the tech/crew does at the end of every job (photos, notes, invoice, payment)
4. **Customer Complaint Handling** — Who handles it, how, and in what timeframe
5. **New Employee Onboarding** — First 30 days for a field tech or office role
6. **Invoice and Accounts Receivable** — How invoices are sent and collections handled
7. **Weekly Reporting Pull** — How data gets compiled for the weekly leadership review

Start here. Add more as the business scales.

## SOP Template Structure

Every SOP should use the same template. Consistency matters for adoption.

### Header Block
```
SOP Title: [Name of process]
Version: 1.0
Last Updated: [Date]
Owner: [Name of role responsible for maintaining this SOP]
Applies To: [Which roles/departments use this]
```

### Purpose (2–3 sentences)
Why this process exists and what problem it solves. Skip this section and people won't understand why they're following the steps.

### Trigger / When to Use
What event starts this process? (e.g., "Use this SOP when a customer calls in to book a new service appointment.")

### Required Tools / Access
What software, logins, forms, or materials are needed before starting?

### Step-by-Step Process
Numbered list, not bullets. Each step should be one action. If a step has a decision branch, use this format:
```
Step 5: If customer requests same-day service:
   → YES: Proceed to Emergency Dispatch SOP
   → NO: Continue to Step 6
```

### Common Errors / What Not to Do
1–3 bullet points of the most frequent mistakes people make in this process and why they matter.

### Quality Check
How do you verify this process was done correctly? (e.g., "Review dispatch board daily at 8 AM to confirm all scheduled jobs have tech assignments.")

### Related SOPs
Links to upstream and downstream processes.

## SOP Length Guidelines

| Process Type | Target Length |
|---|---|
| Simple task (single role, 5–10 steps) | 1 page |
| Multi-step operational process | 2–3 pages |
| Cross-functional process (multiple roles) | 3–5 pages with flowchart |
| Training guide / onboarding | 5–10 pages |

If an SOP exceeds 5 pages, it's either covering too much ground or needs to be split. Break it into sub-SOPs.

## Tools for Storing and Sharing SOPs

### Recommended Options (Ranked)

**1. Notion (Best for most companies)**
Organized wiki with nested pages, easy linking between SOPs, searchable, embeddable videos, mobile-accessible. Free tier is sufficient for small teams. $8–$16/month per user for team features.

**2. Google Sites or Google Drive (Low friction)**
If the team already lives in Google Workspace, a simple Drive folder structure is adequate. No extra tools to learn. Weaker on organization at scale.

**3. Process Street or Trainual (Purpose-built)**
Designed specifically for SOP management with built-in checklists, training modules, and accountability tracking. $99–$300/month. Overkill for a company under 15 employees, strong for 20+.

**4. ServiceTitan / FSM built-in (For operational SOPs)**
Some FSMs allow attaching job checklists to job types. This is the most effective delivery method for field SOPs — the checklist appears on the job in the app the tech is already using.

**Avoid:** Emailing SOPs as PDFs. They become instantly outdated. Use a living document system.

## Training on SOPs

Writing the SOP is 20% of the work. Getting people to use it is the other 80%.

### Initial Training Protocol
1. **Walk through it live** — manager reviews the SOP with the employee on-screen, not just hands them a link
2. **Have them do it while you watch** — supervised execution of the process once
3. **Knowledge check** — 3 questions to confirm they understand the "why" not just the steps
4. **Sign-off** — employee signs that they've been trained on this SOP (logged in their personnel file or HR system)

### Reinforcement
- Reference SOPs in weekly meetings: "Remember, for job closeout we follow the SOP — let's review step 5 this week"
- When someone deviates, use the SOP as the accountability reference: "Walk me through the job closeout SOP steps — where did we go off-track?"
- Update SOPs when the process changes. An outdated SOP is worse than no SOP — it trains people into wrong behavior.

### Video SOPs
For complex processes, a 3–5 minute screen-recording or walkthrough video embedded in the SOP dramatically improves adoption, especially for less text-oriented field staff. Tools: Loom (free tier available).

## SOP Audit Cadence

| Frequency | Action |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Review any SOP tied to a recent complaint or error |
| Quarterly | Owner or ops manager reviews 20–30% of SOPs for accuracy |
| Annually | Full SOP library audit — delete outdated docs, update all headers |

SOPs that haven't been reviewed in 12+ months should be flagged. If a process changed and the SOP didn't, the SOP will actively mislead staff.

## SOP Quick Wins (Can Be Done in Week 1)

If a client has zero SOPs and needs to show progress fast, these three can be drafted and deployed in 3–5 business days:

1. **Job Closeout Checklist** — What the tech/crew does before leaving a job site (photos, materials list, customer signature, invoice sent)
2. **Customer Complaint Response** — Who handles it, how to document it, what authority they have to resolve it
3. **New Lead Response** — Who responds to inbound leads, how fast, with what message

These three alone will reduce callbacks, improve customer satisfaction scores, and start building a culture of documentation.
