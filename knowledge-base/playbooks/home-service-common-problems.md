# Top 10 Operational Failures in Home Service Companies

These are the problems a fractional COO is hired to solve. Understanding them by name — including what causes them and what they cost — is the foundation of the diagnostic.

---

## Problem 1: Scheduling and Dispatch Chaos

**What it looks like:** Technicians don't know their schedule until the morning. Jobs are double-booked or under-scheduled. Customers are given 4-hour windows and still have to call to find out if someone is coming. Dispatchers are reactive, not proactive.

**Root cause:** No FSM software, or FSM software that isn't being used properly. No dispatcher accountability metrics. Jobs being scheduled in a spreadsheet or whiteboard.

**What it costs:** 15–25% capacity loss from inefficient routing. Technician turnover from frustration. Customer churn from poor experience. Callbacks from jobs that were rushed.

**The fix:** Proper FSM implementation (ServiceTitan, Jobber, or equivalent), dispatch SOP, and daily scheduling rhythm. Usually achievable in 30–60 days with the right tool and training.

---

## Problem 2: No Standard Operating Procedures

**What it looks like:** Everything lives in the owner's head. Every employee does the same task differently. When a key person leaves, their process leaves with them. Onboarding new hires takes 3–6 months because there's nothing written down.

**Root cause:** "We've always done it this way" culture. Owner hasn't prioritized documentation because it's not urgent until someone quits.

**What it costs:** Training time, quality inconsistency, customer experience variance, dependency on specific people. This is the single biggest barrier to scaling.

**The fix:** SOP development starting with the 3–5 most-repeated processes (dispatch, job closeout, estimate process, customer communication). See `sop-framework.md`.

---

## Problem 3: Poor Cash Flow Visibility

**What it looks like:** Owner checks the bank account to see if they can make payroll. Cash feels tight even in high-revenue months. No one knows what's coming in or going out beyond the next week.

**Root cause:** Revenue recognition confusion (especially with deposits and insurance jobs), no cash flow forecast, no separation of operating cash from working capital.

**What it costs:** Stress, bad decisions under pressure, line of credit dependency, missed growth opportunities. Seasonal companies hit this hardest.

**The fix:** 13-week rolling cash flow model, draw schedule discipline, QBO cleanup, and weekly cash flow review. See `cash-flow-management.md`.

---

## Problem 4: No KPI Tracking or Accountability

**What it looks like:** Owner has a general sense of revenue but can't tell you close rate, cost per lead, gross margin by job, or technician productivity. Managers report that things are "going well" but there's no data to verify.

**Root cause:** No dashboard, no reporting cadence, no accountability culture. The company was built on hustle, not metrics.

**What it costs:** Profit leakage that goes undetected for months. Inability to identify underperformers. No feedback loop for process improvement.

**The fix:** Build a KPI dashboard (can be a Google Sheet before anything fancier) with 8–12 metrics, reviewed weekly. See `home-service-kpi-library.md`.

---

## Problem 5: High Technician and Field Staff Turnover

**What it looks like:** Average tech tenure under 18 months. Constant recruiting, constant training, constant callbacks from new hires making mistakes. Owner is always in "replace mode."

**Root cause:** No defined career path, inconsistent pay structure, poor onboarding, lack of recognition, and managers who haven't been taught to manage.

**What it costs:** Industry estimates $3,000–$10,000 to replace a field technician when you account for recruiting, training, lost productivity, and callbacks. At 30% annual turnover in a 10-tech shop, that's $90K–$300K/year in hidden cost.

**The fix:** Documented career ladder, structured onboarding, consistent pay/bonus structure, and manager accountability. Not fast or cheap — this is a 6–12 month problem.

---

## Problem 6: The Owner Is the Bottleneck

**What it looks like:** Nothing moves without the owner's sign-off. Owner is on every job site, in every sales call, approving every discount. The business cannot function if the owner takes a week off.

**Root cause:** The owner built the company on personal execution and never built delegation systems. Usually not a trust problem — it's a documentation and accountability infrastructure problem.

**What it costs:** Owner burnout, missed growth, inability to scale. The company is worth much less at acquisition if it's completely owner-dependent.

**The fix:** Role clarity, documented decision authority (who can approve what up to what dollar amount), weekly reporting rhythms. This is typically the #1 thing a fractional COO addresses first.

---

## Problem 7: Inconsistent Sales Process

**What it looks like:** Every salesperson (or the owner) runs estimates differently. No standardized pricing tool. Discounts are given ad hoc. Win/loss is tracked informally. Close rates vary 30 points across reps.

**Root cause:** Sales was built by whoever was selling, with no standardized process. CRM may exist but isn't being used. No sales manager, or a sales manager who also sells.

**What it costs:** Margin erosion from undisciplined discounting. Revenue inconsistency. Good salespeople leaving because commission isn't predictable.

**The fix:** Standardized estimate template, pricing tool with minimum margins built in, CRM usage mandated with pipeline stages defined. Close rate tracking starts immediately.

---

## Problem 8: Uncontrolled Job Costs

**What it looks like:** The company is doing $6M in revenue and only making 6% net profit. Materials come in over budget consistently. Labor hours exceed estimates. No one knows which jobs made money until the accountant closes the books 6 weeks later.

**Root cause:** Estimates built on gut feel, not data. No job costing system. Materials tracked at the company level, not the job level. No post-job review process.

**What it costs:** The difference between 8% net margin and 14% net margin on $6M is $360,000/year. This is usually the single biggest financial opportunity.

**The fix:** FSM-based job costing, estimating templates with labor/material cost built in, weekly job cost review. Takes 60–90 days to get clean data flowing.

---

## Problem 9: No Recruiting or Hiring System

**What it looks like:** Hiring happens when someone quits. No job descriptions that are current. Interviews are conversations, not evaluations. Every hiring decision is gut feel. Bad hires are kept too long because firing is uncomfortable.

**Root cause:** The company was small enough that the owner knew everyone personally. Hiring was simple when there were 5 people. It doesn't work at 25.

**What it costs:** Bad hires average 1.5–2x annual salary in total cost (recruiting, onboarding, underperformance, eventual termination). For a $60K/year tech, that's $90K–$120K per bad hire.

**The fix:** Job descriptions with required outcomes (not just duties), structured interview scorecards, 30/60/90 day onboarding with clear checkpoints.

---

## Problem 10: Technology Sprawl or Technology Avoidance

**What it looks like:** Either (a) the company has 6 different software subscriptions that don't talk to each other and no one is using any of them fully, or (b) everything is still done on paper, spreadsheets, and phone calls.

**Root cause:** Software purchased reactively to solve individual problems without system thinking. Or: tech aversion because the previous attempt failed.

**What it costs:** Version (a): $1,500–$5,000/month in unused software subscriptions plus double data entry. Version (b): 10–20% capacity loss from manual processes that could be automated.

**The fix:** Tech stack audit starting with the FSM (the operational core). Consolidate to the smallest number of tools that cover the full workflow. Implement fully before adding anything new. See `tech-stack-home-service.md`.
