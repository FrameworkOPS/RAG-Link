# LinkedIn Positioning for a Fractional COO in Home Service

## Positioning Principle

LinkedIn for a fractional COO is not about broadcasting credentials — it's about demonstrating specific, earned expertise in a narrow niche. "Former COO with 10 years of experience" means nothing to a roofing company owner. "Here's how I cut a roofing company's callback rate by 40% in 60 days" is a conversation starter.

The goal is not to go viral. The goal is to become the person that home service operators follow and eventually reach out to when the pain gets bad enough. Most buyers will watch for 3–6 months before taking action.

---

## Profile Optimization

### Headline
Do not use a job title. Use a problem-solution frame.

**Weak:** "Fractional COO | Operations Consultant | MBA"
**Strong:** "I build operating systems for home service companies that are growing faster than their processes | Fractional COO @ Framework OPS"

Alternative: "Helping roofing, HVAC, and trades companies get out of the owner-is-the-bottleneck trap | Fractional COO"

### About Section
Structure: Hook → Credibility → Specific expertise → Who you help → Call to action

**Hook:** "Most home service companies between $3M and $10M are run by brilliant operators who've outgrown their own systems."

**Credibility:** Current COO at a large roofing company, 10+ years of org leadership, BBA/MBA.

**Specific expertise:** Mention ServiceTitan, job costing, crew management, cash flow, SOPs — the actual vocabulary of the industry.

**Who you help:** Named industries. Named revenue range. Named symptoms.

**CTA:** One clear next step. "If you're in the weeds and want to talk about what a fractional COO engagement looks like, connect and send me a message."

### Featured Section
Use this strategically. Best options:
- Link to a diagnostic or assessment tool (free value, lead capture)
- Link to a LinkedIn article or newsletter post with high engagement
- Link to the Framework OPS website (when live and credible)

### Experience
Current role at the roofing company should show seniority and scope — mention team size, revenue responsibility, specific achievements. This is the proof of live-fire experience.

### Creator Mode
Enable it. Sets a "Follow" button as primary action, which grows reach faster than connection-only.

## Content Pillars

Post consistently in 3–4 content areas. Mixing these prevents the profile from feeling one-note.

### Pillar 1: Industry Benchmarks and Data (35% of content)
Examples:
- "The average roofing company's gross margin is X. Here's what the top quartile looks like and the 3 differences between them."
- "5 HVAC KPIs every operator should be tracking. Most track zero of these."
- "Industry data: what callback rates actually look like across residential service companies."

Why it works: Owners validate their own situation against benchmarks. This positions you as the authoritative source on what "good" looks like.

### Pillar 2: Stories from the Field (30% of content)
Examples:
- "We discovered a $340K/year cost leak in a client's job costing. Here's exactly how we found it."
- "A roofing company owner told me he was on the verge of burning out. 90 days later, here's what changed."
- "Onboarded a new client last month. First thing I noticed: they had ServiceTitan but were still dispatching from a whiteboard."

Why it works: Specific and real. This is what builds trust. Abstract expertise claims don't convert — concrete examples do. Keep the company anonymous but the details specific.

### Pillar 3: Tactical How-To Content (25% of content)
Examples:
- "How to build a 13-week cash flow model in a spreadsheet (and why every home service operator should have one)"
- "The job closeout SOP we use for every new client. 8 steps. Steal it."
- "If you run a home service company and want to track 5 KPIs instead of 50, start with these."

Why it works: Gives genuine value. Owners save the post, share it, or DM you. This is the lead generation engine.

### Pillar 4: Point of View / Contrarian Takes (10% of content)
Examples:
- "ServiceTitan is not the answer. Here's why most companies that buy it don't see ROI."
- "The business coach market in home service is full of people who've never actually operated a home service company."
- "Hiring a full-time COO at $5M in revenue is usually a mistake. Here's what to do instead."

Why it works: Generates debate, shares, and comments. Positions you as someone with a real viewpoint, not just a hired hand.

## Posting Frequency and Format

**Frequency:** 3–5 posts per week. Consistency beats brilliance. The algorithm rewards regularity.

**Best formats for B2B organic reach (ranked):**
1. Text-only posts (counterintuitively, LinkedIn's algorithm often favors these)
2. Carousel posts (multiple images — high saves and shares)
3. Short videos (2–4 minutes, face-to-camera, no production required)
4. Articles/newsletters (slower to gain traction but strong for SEO and long-term credibility)
5. Polls (good for engagement, not for brand building)

**Avoid:** Posting only promotional content ("We help companies scale — book a call!"). Ratio should be 80% educational/story content, 20% or less explicit positioning.

---

## Engagement Strategy

Posting alone isn't enough. The algorithm surfaces content from people who engage with you.

**Daily engagement routine (15 minutes):**
- Comment substantively (2–3 sentences, not just "great post") on 5–7 posts from target ICP owners
- Reply to every comment on your own posts within 4 hours
- DM new followers who fit the ICP — not with a pitch, with a genuine question or compliment

**Connection building:**
- Connect with roofing/HVAC/plumbing company owners directly (search by job title + industry)
- Connect with referral partners: trade CPAs, business bankers, insurance brokers, supplier reps
- Do not mass-connect without a personalized note

## Credibility Signals to Build Over Time

- LinkedIn newsletter in the home service operations niche (builds subscriber base)
- LinkedIn "Top Voice" badge (follow platform best practices over 90 days)
- Testimonials/recommendations from clients (request after first successful engagement milestone)
- Speaking appearances at industry associations (NRCA, ACCA) — mention and link on profile
- Podcast appearances (creates content assets and backlinks)

---

## What to Avoid

- Do not post motivational content ("Monday mindset: success is a choice") — wrong audience, wrong brand
- Do not post about anything unrelated to home service operations or fractional COO work until there's a strong following (and even then, be careful)
- Do not start with a paid advertising strategy — organic content builds trust. Ads without proven organic content just buy impressions from skeptical operators
- Do not post AI-generated content that reads like AI — this audience is hands-on and will spot it
