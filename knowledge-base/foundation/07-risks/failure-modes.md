# Failure Modes

Known risks to watch. Each has a mitigation. Review at every Sunday checkpoint.

## 1. Infinite preparation trap ⚠️ HIGHEST RISK

**Pattern:** Disorganized founder over-builds pre-revenue. Always "one more week" until ready.

**Symptoms:**
- Pushing launch date even slightly
- Adding "just one more" item to a week's deliverables
- Polish without specific user need driving it
- Feeling unready despite hitting milestones

**Mitigation:**
- Hard launch date on calendar: July 5, 2026 (locked)
- Weekly checkpoint forces honest done/not-done
- 80% rule per [[build-philosophy]]
- Build-in-public on LinkedIn = visible accountability

**Failure cost:** Every 1 month delay = $8-15K Year 1 revenue loss per [[revenue-targets]]

## 2. Daniel engagement expanding to consume all available time

**Pattern:** Active client work expands to fill schedule. Framework OPS infrastructure builds slip.

**Symptoms:**
- Daniel sessions running long
- Out-of-scope requests accepted "just this once"
- Framework OPS hours dropping below 20/week
- Skipping Sunday checkpoints

**Mitigation:**
- Cap weekly Daniel hours (target: ~10/week including session + prep + async)
- Enforce scope per [[scope-management]]
- Use Daniel engagement as test case for engagement template generator, not as time sink

**Failure cost:** Daniel becomes the only client through Q3 2026 = $40-50K cumulative through EOY 2027, missing target by $100K+

## 3. Claude Code projects taking 2-3x estimates

**Pattern:** API integrations, edge cases, auth complexity, debugging time always exceeds initial estimate.

**Symptoms:**
- Week 3 ending with engagement generator at 30% complete
- "I'll catch up next week" without scope cut
- New libraries/approaches mid-build

**Mitigation:**
- Cut scope BEFORE sliding launch
- Phase 2 functionality moves to [[deferred-to-phase-2]]
- 80% solution > polished half-built thing
- If week 3 is 50% behind, accept that engagement generator ships in late Week 5

**Failure cost:** Either launch slips OR launch happens without core tools = both bad

## 4. Underpricing trap without case study capture

**Pattern:** Aggressive pricing only builds reputation if testimonials/case studies actually get captured. Without contractual enforcement, clients ghost the post-mortem.

**Symptoms:**
- Daniel engagement ends without written testimonial
- No case study interview scheduled
- Promised "I'll get to it" turns into never

**Mitigation:**
- Mandatory case study clause in every SOW per [[contract-requirements]]
- Schedule case study interview at engagement kickoff, not end
- Pre-write testimonial draft, send for editing (lowers friction)
- Don't deliver final docs/access until case study captured

**Failure cost:** Aggressive pricing without case studies = aggressive pricing with no reputation upside

## 5. Cheap clients = highest scrutiny clients

**Pattern:** $1-3M owners writing small checks scrutinize every hour. More hand-holding than expected.

**Symptoms:**
- Hours blowing out on smaller engagements
- Defensive emails defending value
- Owner second-guessing every recommendation

**Mitigation:**
- Build extra hand-holding time into pricing assumptions
- Strong SOW + change order enforcement per [[scope-management]]
- Set communication SLAs in writing
- Fire bad clients early (see [[scope-management]])

**Failure cost:** Time blowing out = effective hourly rate dropping below roofing job rate

## 6. LinkedIn over-investment with no payoff

**Pattern:** Putting 10+ hours/week into LinkedIn when realistic 60-day outcome is zero inbound leads.

**Symptoms:**
- Posting taking more time than direct outreach
- Tweaking content endlessly
- Checking metrics multiple times per day
- Avoiding direct outreach because LinkedIn "feels productive"

**Mitigation:**
- Cap LinkedIn time per [[linkedin-status]]: 3-5 hrs/week
- LinkedIn is proof layer, NOT pipeline
- Pipeline comes from warm network + direct outreach
- Track conversations not vanity metrics

**Failure cost:** 60 days of posting → zero pipeline = launch happens with empty calendar

## 7. Sub-vertical decision paralysis

**Pattern:** Trying to serve all home services creates generic positioning that resonates with nobody.

**Symptoms:**
- ICP list spans 4+ sub-verticals
- Messaging hedges between verticals
- No specific "I work with [sub-vertical]" tagline
- Outreach feels generic

**Mitigation:**
- Decision deadline: end of Week 1 (May 17)
- Recommended: Roofing-only first (strongest network + credibility)
- Can expand later; cannot un-narrow

**Failure cost:** Discovery calls converting at half the rate they could

## Weekly checkpoint questions

Every Sunday 7pm PT, ask:

1. Am I in the infinite prep trap? (How many launch-relevant items shipped this week vs. nice-to-haves?)
2. Did Daniel hours stay capped?
3. Are my Claude Code builds on schedule, or do I need to cut scope?
4. Did I capture case study evidence this week (if applicable)?
5. Am I spending more time on LinkedIn than direct outreach?
6. Is my sub-vertical focus still tight?
