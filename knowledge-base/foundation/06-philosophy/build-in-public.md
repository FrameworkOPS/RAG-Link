# Build in Public

Marketing and content strategy. Why every internal build is also content.

## Core thesis

For a fractional COO selling operational discipline, **showing the work is the strongest possible marketing.**

Most fractional COOs talk about systems. Few show them. Showing builds trust faster than talking ever will.

## Why this works specifically for Framework OPS

1. **Differentiation:** The category is full of generic "process and people" advice. Specifics stand out.
2. **Credibility:** Sitting COO at large roofing company = unique vantage point. Show it.
3. **Audience match:** $1-3M home service owners are operators. They respect operators who build.
4. **Compounding value:** Every internal build doubles as content asset.

## Content angle priority

### 1. Build-in-public (highest priority)
What you're building for Framework OPS itself.
- ClickUp command center setup → screenshots + rationale
- Engagement template generator → demo video + code snippets
- N8N flows → workflow diagrams + use cases
- SOPs → format breakdowns + examples

### 2. Operator POV (highest credibility moat)
Insights from the sitting COO seat. Use aggressively before leaving the role.
- "What actually breaks at $5M+ roofing companies"
- "The real reason your techs aren't documenting properly"
- "What I see operators get wrong in their first hire after $1M"

### 3. Tactical breakdowns
Real numbers, real mechanisms.
- "How we cut onboarding from 4 hours to 40 minutes"
- "The 3 ClickUp custom fields that prevent 80% of dropped leads"
- "Why most home service businesses don't need a CRM"

### 4. Case studies
Once Daniel case study is live, frame variations:
- The audit findings
- The implementation approach
- The before/after metrics
- The lessons learned

## What NOT to post

- Generic leadership quotes ("Leaders lead")
- AI hype posts ("AI is changing everything!")
- Motivational content
- "5 lessons I learned" listicles without specifics
- Industry trend takes without operator perspective
- Personal life content (unless tied to business lesson)
- Hot takes for engagement bait

The home service owner audience smells fluff immediately and most of LinkedIn is fluff. Don't add to it.

## Format guidelines

- **Length:** 150-400 words sweet spot. Long enough to say something, short enough to read.
- **Structure:** Hook (1 line) → context (2-3 lines) → meat (5-15 lines) → close (1-2 lines).
- **Visuals:** Screenshots > diagrams > stock images > nothing. Real artifacts only.
- **Comments:** Engage with replies within 4 hours of posting when possible. Comments fuel reach.

## Cadence

- **Posts:** 3-4/week minimum, 5-7/week target post-launch
- **Comments on others:** 10 ICP-adjacent comments/day
- **DMs:** 5-10 personalized DMs/week to new connections

## Tracking metrics

Per [[clickup-hierarchy]] Marketing & Content space:

- Engagement count (likes, comments, reposts)
- Comments count specifically (most predictive of reach)
- Connection requests received
- Profile views
- Leads generated (rare but track)

**What matters most:** Conversations started, not vanity metrics.

## Repurposing strategy

Every post should have a "where else this lives" plan:

- LinkedIn post → email signature line
- LinkedIn post → blog post (expanded version)
- LinkedIn carousel → PDF lead magnet
- Build-in-public post → sales page evidence
- Case study post → website case study page
