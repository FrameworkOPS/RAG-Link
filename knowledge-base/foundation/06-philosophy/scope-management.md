# Scope Management

Hard-won lessons from the Daniel engagement, codified.

## Core rule

**Verbal scope additions are NOT accepted.**

Every change to scope requires written change order before work begins.

## The trap

Owners default to assuming you'll do "whatever needs doing." They'll say:
- "While you're in there, can you also..."
- "I know it's not in scope but..."
- "Quick question — can you just..."
- "It should only take you a minute to..."

Every one of these is a scope creep moment. They feel small individually. They destroy engagement margin in aggregate.

## The response

> "Happy to scope that. Let me send a change order today so we can keep the original SOW clean. Should take me 10 minutes."

This response:
- Doesn't say no
- Doesn't shame the owner
- Reasserts the SOW as the source of truth
- Creates explicit decision point
- Often results in owner saying "actually, never mind"

## Change order policy

Per [[contract-requirements]]:

- $250/hour for hourly work, OR
- $2,000 per added workflow/SOP/automation

Whichever is greater.

**Always in writing.** Email + signature acceptable, but no work starts until signed.

## Out-of-scope language in every SOW

Specific examples of what's NOT included must be listed. Generic items:

- Ongoing operational management after engagement
- Direct team management (hiring, firing, performance reviews)
- Financial controllership
- Legal advice or contract review
- Recruiting services (vs. hiring framework support)
- Additional automations beyond agreed count
- Additional SOPs beyond agreed count
- Software purchases or vendor negotiations
- Marketing or sales execution

## Capping hours on retainers

The OPS Embedded tier originally priced at $6,500/30-40 hrs was the worst hourly rate on the offer ladder. Re-priced to $5,500/25-30 hrs.

**Rule:** When pricing retainers, watch effective hourly rate. The most expensive tier should NOT have the lowest hourly rate.

Per [[offer-ladder]] all retainers now in $175-220/hr effective range.

## Time tracking

Even for fixed-fee productized work, track hours per engagement. Reasons:

1. Detect scope creep early
2. Validate pricing decisions
3. Inform Year 2 price increases
4. Identify which clients are profitable vs. money pits

Tool: ClickUp time tracking in Client Delivery space.

## When to fire a client

Rare but necessary. Triggers:

- Repeated scope creep after multiple change orders enforced
- Late/missed payments (after 1 warning)
- Disrespect of working session structure (constant cancellations, no-shows)
- Asking for things explicitly out-of-scope and refusing change orders

Process:
1. Written notice citing specific contractual issues
2. 30-day cure period (per cancellation terms in [[contract-requirements]])
3. If unresolved: written termination, refund per cancellation terms

## Lesson from Daniel engagement

Daniel engagement specifically taught the scope creep lesson — that's why scope management guardrails became a deliverable in the [[active-clients]] file.

Carry forward: every new engagement starts with explicit conversation about change order policy in kickoff. Don't wait until first creep moment.
