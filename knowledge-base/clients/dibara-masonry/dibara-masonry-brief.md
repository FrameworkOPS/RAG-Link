# Project Brief: DiBara Masonry

## Client Overview

- **Client:** CEO/Owner (name to confirm)
- **Company:** DiBara Masonry — masonry and concrete contractor
- **Model:** Specialty trade; mix of in-house crews and sub labor. Residential/commercial split unconfirmed.
- **Structure:** Owner-led (Matt). Sales: 1 inside sales manager + 3 inside reps + 2 field sales techs (6 total). Production: production manager + experienced PM + a couple in-house crews + subs. Finance: bookkeeping (separate role/person) with weekly review cadence including Matt and production.

## ICP Fit Assessment

**Fit: Strong (8/10).** Within $2M–$20M ICP. Owner-operator. Multiple buying triggers present: no job costing, owner-approves-everything in sales, near-miss hiring pattern, recent software investment with no operator behind it.

**Disqualifier check:** Clear on revenue floor (well above $1M), no evidence of consultant carousel, owner appears to be implementation-willing (looking to "set it up and run it" — not "just tell me what to do"), not in active turnaround (growing). One soft note: masonry/concrete is a specialty trade, not a "home service" trade in the strict sense — playbook benchmarks (roofing, HVAC) apply directionally, not exactly.

## Working Context

**Tone & framing:** Trade operator language. Masonry/concrete vocabulary (sub labor, work orders, job costing at the phase level, draw schedules if commercial). The owner is a hands-on operator who built the company — speak to him like a peer with deeper systems experience, not like a consultant.

**Priorities in order (proposed — diagnostic will confirm):**
1. **Financial visibility** — job costing + granular COGS. The single highest-ROI fix at $12M. Without it, margin recovery is impossible to measure or defend.
2. **Production CRM + work order ownership** — close the structural gap where subs effectively run the dispatch process. This is where margin leaks.
3. **Owner exit from the sales seat** — capacity unlock, but only AFTER #1 and #2 are stable. Recruiting "killer" sales talent into a broken back end produces churn, not growth.

**Default assumptions:**
- The owner will pay for speed but won't tolerate fluff. Every deliverable must be specific and observable.
- The marketing agency will be territorial about HubSpot — explicit lane-defining at kickoff is mandatory.
- Job costing implementation will reveal that some service lines or job types are unprofitable. The owner needs to be prepared for that conversation before the diagnostic starts.
- Margin recovery in masonry is slow — meaningful change is 6–12 months, not 90 days. Pessimistic ROI math in every projection.

**What to avoid:**
- Don't quote upside ROI without quoting the downside. Per CLAUDE.md.
- Don't recommend a new FSM platform in the diagnostic. Service Hub may absorb the production-CRM gap for now.
- Don't propose hiring solutions for sales until job costing reveals whether the company can afford to add reps.
- Don't position this as a turnaround — DiBara is growing, this is an infrastructure install, not a rescue.
