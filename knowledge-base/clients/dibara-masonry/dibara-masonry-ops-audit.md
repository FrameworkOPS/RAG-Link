## 1. Executive Summary

DiBara is a $12M masonry and concrete contractor in Los Angeles that has built
real revenue without building the operating infrastructure to support it. The
single biggest constraint is **financial blindness at the job level**: the weekly
financial review runs at the P&L level only, so margin cannot be measured, defended,
or recovered job by job. Compounding it, **no one owns the work order** and
**subcontractors procure their own materials** — meaning one of the largest cost
lines in the business sits outside DiBara's direct control. The headline
opportunity is straightforward: install job costing and work-order ownership, and
DiBara converts a $12M top line it cannot see into one it can manage.

Provisional scorecard average (5 of 10 dimensions scored): **2.2 / 5** — early-stage
operational maturity, consistent with a company whose revenue has outgrown its
systems. Weakest dimensions: Sales handoff, Job execution, Owner dependency, and
Hiring — all scored 2.

## 3. Findings by Dimension

### 2. Sales handoff — Score 2
**Assessment.** A marketing agency partner is standing up HubSpot Marketing and
Sales Hubs, so the *sales* side is gaining structure. But the recap is explicit
that the **production-side workflow — job ticketing, customer-facing status,
punch-list — has no home in HubSpot**. The moment a deal is won, it leaves a
system and enters an undocumented manual handoff. Information re-asked, scope lost,
and start delays all live in that gap.

**Benchmark gap.** Margin in construction leaks most often in the gap between
field operations and financial records — miscoded labor, vanished change orders,
billing that lags the work (Adeltium; MarginLock). A systemized sales-to-production
handoff is the structural fix.

**Cost of inaction.** Every undocumented handoff is a re-work and scope-creep risk
on a six-figure masonry job. Unbilled change orders are pure margin loss.

**Recommendations.**
1. Stand up the production workflow — HubSpot Service Hub (the Optional Phase in
   the proposal) or an equivalent — with a defined sales→production handoff record.
2. Define the handoff data set: what sales must capture at close for production to
   start without re-asking.

### 4. Job execution — Score 2
**Assessment.** DiBara delivers $12M of work through a production manager, an
experienced PM, a couple of in-house crews, and primarily sub labor. But two
structural gaps surfaced in discovery: **work-order ownership is undefined** (when
asked who creates and tracks work orders, "the answer wasn't clear" — effectively
no one), and **subs order their own materials**. Materials are 30–40% of project
cost in this trade; DiBara has handed control of that line to its subcontractors.

**Benchmark gap.** Labor and materials together are the dominant cost in masonry,
and material waste on poorly scoped jobs is a top-named margin leak for specialty
trades (Adeltium). Healthy masonry/concrete gross margin sits in the **20–30%**
range — unreachable without controlling material procurement.

**Cost of inaction.** Uncontrolled material procurement on a $12M base is the
single largest unmanaged cost in the business. At LA material and labor pricing,
even a few points of waste is six figures a year.

**Recommendations.**
1. Assign explicit work-order ownership — one named role creates and tracks every
   work order from win to closeout.
2. Bring material procurement in-house, or put it under a documented PO process
   with DiBara approval before subs order.

### 8. Hiring / onboarding — Score 2
**Assessment.** Evidence is limited to *sales* hiring: hiring "killer" reps has
been hard enough that the owner is considering relocation, and the brief notes a
near-miss hiring pattern. Hiring appears reactive, with no documented onboarding
process evident. No data yet on production/field hiring or ramp time — the
diagnostic must close this.

**Benchmark gap.** Owner-led reactive hiring with no onboarding system is the
default failure mode for $1–20M contractors. The Framework OPS recommendation is
*not* another hire — it is a rigid, documented sales training program (scripts,
scorecards, ride-along cadence) so the existing 6-person team and the next hire
ramp predictably.

**Cost of inaction.** Hiring into a broken back end produces churn, not growth.
Relocation spend to import a "killer" rep is high-risk while the handoff and
job-costing systems are still missing.

**Recommendations.**
1. Build the documented sales training program before any further sales hire.
2. Defer the relocation/hiring decision until job costing reveals whether the unit
   economics support added headcount.

### 9. Financial close — Score 3
**Assessment.** This is DiBara's genuine strength. A **weekly financial cadence**
already exists, with the owner, bookkeeping, and production at the table — more
than most $12M contractors have. But it is **reactive and P&L-level**: there is no
job-level costing, no margin by service line or crew, and the meeting reports on
what already happened rather than driving the week ahead.

**Benchmark gap.** Contractors who track actual costs against estimate in real
time achieve **15–25% better margins** than those who cost jobs only at completion
(Autodesk; Projul). Healthy masonry/concrete gross margin is **20–30%**; DiBara's
trailing-12-month gross margin is currently unconfirmed (open question in the
brief) — and without job costing the company cannot place itself on that range or
defend it. At $12M, **each single point of gross margin is ~$120K**.

**Cost of inaction.** Margin recovery is impossible to measure or defend without
job-level data. The company is flying a $12M aircraft on a fuel gauge that only
reads at the end of the month.

**Recommendations.**
1. Install job costing — actual vs. estimated cost at the job and phase level.
2. Upgrade the weekly review from reactive P&L reporting to a forward-looking job
   margin scorecard plus a rolling cash forecast.

### 10. Owner dependency — Score 2
**Assessment.** The sales team is in place — 1 inside sales manager, 3 inside reps,
2 field sales techs — yet **every deal still touches the owner's desk**. Sales runs
through Matt and only through Matt. He wants to step out of the sales seat; today
the business cannot let him.

**Benchmark gap.** Owner-as-bottleneck is the defining constraint for contractors
at this stage. The unlock is process, not headcount: a documented sales system the
team can run without the owner approving every deal.

**Cost of inaction.** The owner's capacity is the ceiling on growth. Until deals
stop routing through him, DiBara cannot scale sales beyond his personal bandwidth.

**Recommendations.**
1. Document the sales process end to end so deals can close without owner approval.
2. Sequence the owner's exit from sales *after* job costing and work-order
   ownership are stable — not before.

## 4. Prioritized Recommendations

Ranked by impact — dollar cost and constraint on the owner's goals — not by ease.

1. **Install job costing (Financial close).** Highest-ROI fix at $12M. Real-time
   job costing is worth 15–25% margin improvement industry-wide; each point of
   gross margin is ~$120K here. Everything else is measured against this. *Quick
   to start, 6–12 months to mature.*
2. **Assign work-order ownership + control material procurement (Job execution).**
   Closes the largest unmanaged cost line in the business. *Structural — start
   immediately.*
3. **Stand up the production workflow / sales→production handoff (Sales handoff).**
   The HubSpot Service Hub Optional Phase, or equivalent. *Quick win if Service
   Hub is elected.*
4. **Build the documented sales training program (Hiring + Owner dependency).**
   Unlocks the owner from the sales seat — but only after #1 and #2 are stable.
   *Structural.*
5. **Upgrade the weekly review to forward-looking (Financial close).** Job margin
   scorecard + rolling cash forecast. *Quick win once job costing data exists.*

**Quick wins:** production workflow build, weekly-review upgrade.
**Structural:** job costing, material procurement control, sales training program.

## 5. 90-Day Roadmap

**Days 1–30 — See the business.**
- Complete the Phase 0 diagnostic and score the five pending dimensions.
- Confirm trailing-12-month gross margin and residential/commercial mix.
- Stand up job-costing structure: chart of accounts rebuilt for job-level COGS.
- Name the work-order owner; document the work-order lifecycle.

**Days 31–60 — Control the costs.**
- Move material procurement under a DiBara-approved PO process.
- Launch the production workflow (Service Hub if elected) with a defined handoff.
- First job-cost reports run on live jobs.

**Days 61–90 — Drive decisions.**
- Convert the weekly review to a forward-looking job margin scorecard + rolling
  cash forecast.
- Roll out v1 of the documented sales training program to the existing team.
- Review the first 90 days of job-cost data; identify any unprofitable job types.

## 6. Optional — Scoped Phase 1 Proposal

A Phase 1 Active Retainer ($4,000/mo, month-to-month) is the natural follow-on:
job-costing install, COGS rebuild, KPI dashboard, work-order and procurement SOPs,
the sales training program, and the upgraded weekly review. The HubSpot Service
Hub Build ($3,500) runs concurrent if elected. Pricing per the existing DiBara
proposal — kept proportional to the assessment's scope.
