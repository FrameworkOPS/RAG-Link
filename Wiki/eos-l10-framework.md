# EOS / L10 Meeting Framework

## What EOS Is

The Entrepreneurial Operating System (EOS) is a business operating framework designed for small-to-mid-sized companies. It organizes the leadership of a company around six core components: Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, and Traction. The Level 10 (L10) meeting is the weekly accountability heartbeat of EOS.

Chance Peare runs L10 meetings with client leadership teams as a core deliverable in fractional COO engagements. The L10 is the most visible recurring artifact of the fractional COO relationship — it's how the leadership team stays aligned week to week on what matters.

---

## The Six Components of EOS

### 1. Vision
Everyone in the company rowing in the same direction. Captured in the Vision/Traction Organizer (V/TO):
- Core Values (who we are)
- Core Focus (what we do and why)
- 10-Year Target (long-horizon big goal)
- Marketing Strategy (ICP, uniques, proven process, guarantee)
- 3-Year Picture (specific measurable picture of the company in 3 years)
- 1-Year Plan (annual goals — usually 3–7 SMART goals)
- Quarterly Rocks (the 3–7 most important priorities for the next 90 days)
- Issues List (company-level items to address)

### 2. People
Right people, right seats. EOS uses two tools:
- **Accountability Chart** (not an org chart — shows who is accountable for what function, not just hierarchy)
- **People Analyzer** (evaluating whether each person on the team shares the Core Values and is in the right seat: GWC — Gets it, Wants it, has the Capacity to do it)

### 3. Data
Running the business on numbers, not feelings. The Scorecard is the primary tool:
- 5–15 weekly metrics that give a pulse on the business
- Each metric has a weekly goal (target)
- Green = on track, Red = off track
- Scorecard issues get added to the Issues List for resolution

### 4. Issues
A safe, efficient way to surface and resolve problems. The Issues List is the company's ongoing collection of things that need to be solved. The IDS process (Identify, Discuss, Solve) is the structured method for working through them in L10 meetings.

### 5. Process
The handful of core processes that run the business, documented and followed by everyone. For a home service company, the core processes are typically: Lead Generation, Sales/Estimating, Scheduling/Dispatch, Job Execution, Invoicing/Collections, Hiring/Onboarding.

### 6. Traction
Discipline and accountability. The To-Do list (7-day action items from L10 meetings) and the Rocks process (90-day priorities) are the execution layer.

## The L10 Meeting Format

The Level 10 meeting runs exactly 90 minutes. Same format, every week. The discipline of the format is where the value lives — skipping or modifying segments creates drift.

### Full Agenda (90 Minutes)

| Segment | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Segue** | 5 min | Personal and professional good news. Energy check. No business discussion. |
| **Scorecard Review** | 5 min | Go through each metric. Mark green or red. Don't discuss — just identify reds for the Issues List. |
| **Rock Review** | 5 min | Each Rock owner: on track or off track. No discussion. Off-track Rocks go to the Issues List. |
| **Customer / Employee Headlines** | 5 min | Good news only. Bad news goes to Issues. One sentence per headline. |
| **To-Do List Review** | 5 min | Each 7-day To-Do: done or not done. Not done = discuss why briefly and re-assign or drop. |
| **IDS (Issues Solving)** | 60 min | Work through the Issues List using IDS. Most important issue first. |
| **Conclude** | 5 min | Review new To-Dos, cascading messages (what does the rest of the team need to know?), rate the meeting 1–10. |

**Time discipline is non-negotiable.** An L10 that regularly runs 120 minutes is a broken L10. The facilitator (Chance) manages the clock.

## How Chance Facilitates L10 Meetings

### Before the Meeting
- Confirm the Scorecard has been updated with the prior week's numbers (ideally by EOD Monday for a Tuesday meeting, or morning of for a morning meeting)
- Review which Rocks are approaching their quarterly deadline
- Scan the Issues List for anything that's been sitting unaddressed for more than 2 weeks
- Prepare a pulse check on any outstanding To-Dos from prior weeks

### During the Meeting
- Keep the Segue to 5 minutes — cut it if people start going long
- Call out every red on the Scorecard and add to Issues (don't let the team diagnose reds during Scorecard review — that's what IDS is for)
- Enforce Rock status as binary: on-track or off-track. "Mostly on track" = off-track.
- During IDS: the Identify step is just naming the issue precisely (not diagnosing). The Discuss step is brutal honesty, all perspectives. The Solve step ends with a clear decision and a To-Do with an owner and a 7-day due date.
- Rate the meeting at the end — a well-run L10 should consistently rate 8–10. Below 7 means something in the format is broken.

### After the Meeting
- Update the shared L10 doc with To-Dos, new Rocks progress, and any Rocks that dropped off
- Send the Cascading Message to any team members not in the L10 who need to know decisions made
- Log the meeting summary in ClickUp and/or the Fathom transcript gets ingested into the RAG

## The Scorecard

The Scorecard is the most important EOS tool for a home service company. It answers: "Is the business healthy this week?"

### What Goes on a Home Service Scorecard

**Revenue-side:**
- Revenue invoiced (week and MTD vs. goal)
- Jobs completed
- Close rate (estimates → jobs)
- Number of estimates submitted

**Operational:**
- Gross margin % (if job costing is clean enough for weekly tracking)
- Callbacks / warranty calls (count)
- Crew utilization rate
- Jobs on schedule vs. delayed

**Financial:**
- Cash balance (point-in-time)
- AR >30 days (balance)
- Invoices outstanding >14 days

**Customer/Lead:**
- Leads received (week)
- New Google reviews (week)
- Customer complaints

Each metric gets a weekly target. Consistently red metrics go to Issues.

## Rocks

Rocks are the 3–7 most important priorities for the company and each leader in the next 90 days. They are specific, measurable, and achievable within the quarter.

**What a good Rock looks like:**
- "Complete ServiceTitan onboarding for all dispatch staff by July 15"
- "Document and train team on estimate process SOP by June 30"
- "Reduce DSO from 42 days to 30 days by end of Q2"

**What a bad Rock looks like:**
- "Improve operations"
- "Work on culture"
- "Get better at sales"

Bad Rocks are abstract goals, not measurable outcomes. Chance rewrites them at the quarterly planning session.

### Quarterly Rock Setting (Quarterly Meeting)
Every 90 days, the leadership team runs a full-day (or half-day) Quarterly meeting:
- Review the prior quarter's Rocks (what got done, what didn't, why)
- Update the V/TO if needed
- Set new Rocks for the upcoming 90 days
- Clear the Issues List of anything resolved and re-prioritize what remains

## IDS: Identify, Discuss, Solve

The 60-minute Issues Solving segment is where L10 meetings earn their value. IDS is the method:

**Identify:** State the issue in one sentence. Not the symptom — the root cause. "Our close rate dropped to 28% this month" is a symptom. "We don't have a defined follow-up process after the estimate is sent" is an issue.

**Discuss:** Open the floor. All perspectives on the table. The facilitator ensures everyone is heard and cuts off tangents. Typical discuss time: 3–10 minutes per issue.

**Solve:** Land on a clear decision. Not "we should think about it." A decision with a To-Do owner and a 7-day due date. If the issue can't be solved in the meeting, assign a specific person to come back with a recommendation.

**Issue prioritization:** At the start of the IDS segment, the team numbers the Issues List and votes on the top 3 to work through first. The most important issue for the business gets solved first, not the easiest one.

## Common EOS Problems Chance Fixes

1. **L10 runs long** — Scorecard review or Segue is taking too long. Fix: hard time limits, parking lot for off-topic discussion.
2. **Rocks aren't getting done** — Rocks were aspirational, not realistic. Fix: quarterly reset with tighter scoping.
3. **Issues List grows but never shrinks** — Team avoids difficult conversations. Fix: Chance facilitates the first hard IDS on the elephant-in-the-room issue.
4. **Scorecard has red metrics every week that no one is solving** — Issues aren't being created for reds. Fix: enforce the "red = Issues List" rule without exception.
5. **To-Dos don't get done** — No accountability culture yet. Fix: publicly note undone To-Dos; don't let them roll forever.

## EOS for Home Service Companies

EOS was designed for manufacturing and professional services but translates well to home service. The primary adaptations Chance makes:

- **Scorecard** anchored to field operations metrics (crew utilization, callbacks, close rate) not just revenue
- **Rocks** tied to seasonal realities — a roofing company in Q1 shouldn't have the same Rock priorities as Q3 peak season
- **Accountability Chart** must reflect the real dispatch/ops structure, not a wishful org chart
- **People Analyzer** is most useful during hiring decisions and when a key person is underperforming
