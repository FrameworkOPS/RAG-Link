# Framework OPS LLC — Company Profile

## What Framework OPS Is

Framework OPS LLC is a fractional COO consulting practice founded by Chance Peare. The practice serves owner-operated home service companies in the $2M–$20M revenue range that are growing faster than their operational infrastructure can support. The model is simple: Chance embeds as a part-time Chief Operating Officer, builds the systems the business needs to scale without the owner becoming the bottleneck, and exits with the client running better than they found it.

Framework OPS is newly launched (2026). There is no legacy portfolio, no case studies yet, and no partner network. The value proposition rests on Chance's lived operator experience — he is currently the full-time COO of a large roofing company — and his deliberate lean into AI and automation as a force multiplier for small operations teams.

## Mission

Help owner-operated home service companies build the operational infrastructure to grow without killing the owner. Specific, measurable outcomes: gross margin improvement, owner hours reduction, cash flow visibility, documented processes that survive key person departures.

No vague strategy. No slide decks without implementation. Every engagement ends with systems running in the client's stack, not in a report.

## Ideal Client Profile (ICP)

### Revenue Range
- **$2M–$20M** — the target band
- **Sweet spot: $2M–$8M** (80% of expected pipeline)
- Under $2M: unit economics don't support the engagement fee
- Over $20M: typically needs or has a full-time COO

### Industries
Primary: Roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping/lawn care, pest control, painting, garage door, pool service

Not a fit: Remodeling/GC (different business model), retail, manufacturing, franchise units

### Company Structure
- Founder/owner-operator still active in the business (not absentee)
- 5–75 employees
- At least one full-time office staff member to implement changes with
- Not a franchise (franchisor dictates ops — limited intervention surface)

### Owner Profile
- Age 32–55, built the business through hustle and grit
- Does not consider themselves "a systems person"
- Working 55–70 hrs/week; can't get out of the weeds
- Has bought software the team doesn't use properly
- Gross margin declining as revenue grows — the signature signal of systems failure

### Buying Triggers (3+ = high-likelihood fit)
- Owner approves every discount or estimate personally
- Gross margin declining YoY despite revenue growth
- Same problems recurring month after month
- Near-miss on payroll or a cash flow crisis
- Key person departure that exposed the company
- Software purchased 6+ months ago that isn't used properly
- Owner hasn't taken a real vacation in 2+ years
- Company crossed a growth milestone and operations haven't caught up

### Disqualifiers
- Owner is not the primary decision maker
- 3+ significant consultant engagements in 3 years (implementation resistance)
- Company is actively losing money — this is a turnaround, not an ops upgrade
- Ego-locked owner who will not implement recommendations
- Primary issue is culture/values crisis, not an ops problem

## Differentiators

1. **Lived operator experience, not borrowed expertise.** Chance runs a large roofing company as COO. He knows what a dispatch board looks like at 6 AM, what a ServiceTitan implementation failure costs, and what it takes to close a payroll gap. This is not a strategy firm.

2. **Implementation-first.** Framework OPS delivers working systems, not reports. Proposals name specific deliverables (SOPs, automation workflows, cash flow models, KPI dashboards) with timelines, not vague promises.

3. **AI and automation as a native capability.** Most fractional COOs treat technology as a recommendation. Chance builds it. n8n workflows, Supabase RAG systems, Fathom-to-ClickUp recap pipelines — these run inside his own practice, and he builds the same for clients.

4. **Price point below a full-time hire.** A competent ops manager costs $55K–$80K/year all-in. The Framework OPS Active retainer ($4,000/month) delivers senior COO-level work at $48K/year with no benefits overhead, no severance risk, and no hiring process.

5. **Trade-specific benchmarks.** Framework OPS uses vertical-specific benchmarks (roofing, HVAC, plumbing) to quantify gaps. Telling a roofing company their gross margin is 34% when the benchmark is 43–52% is a different conversation than saying "margins could be better."
